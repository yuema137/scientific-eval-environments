# March 2025 Monthly Report

## Month at a Glance

March 2025 is a framing month rather than a scale month. The agent-evaluation survey, ASIMOV, CoLA, AstroChart, and LLM-EPANET all sharpen the questions of scope, abstraction level, and environment design that later topic expansions in the repository depend on.

## What Changed This Month

### [Scientific Agent Benchmarks](../topics/scientific_agents.md)

This group asks what counts as a defensible scientific benchmark once a field's artifacts, tools, and verification constraints stop looking like generic QA.

- [DomainCQA / AstroChart](../works/domaincqa.md): DomainCQA is a methodology for building knowledge-intensive chart question-answering benchmarks in a scientific domain, instantiated as **AstroChart** — 1,690 QA pairs over 482 charts taken from astronomy papers — and used to evaluate 21 multimodal large language models against a human baseline.
- [LLM-EPANET](../works/llm-epanet.md): LLM-EPANET is a retrieval-augmented, multi-agent pipeline that turns natural-language questions about a water
distribution system into executable EPANET simulation code, released together with a curated benchmark of 69
queries whose ground truth is a hand-written, deterministic reference script for every query.
- [PHYSICS](../works/physics-benchmark.md): PHYSICS is a comprehensive benchmark for university-level physics problem solving: 1,297 expert-annotated problems covering six core areas — classical mechanics, quantum mechanics, thermodynamics and statistical mechanics, electromagnetism, atomic physics, and optics — with a robust automated evaluation system.

### [Survey](../topics/survey.md)

A useful way to read this cluster is to ask which hidden part of evaluation it makes visible.

- [Survey on Evaluation of LLM-based Agents](../works/agent-evaluation-survey.md): *Survey on Evaluation of LLM-based Agents* is a comprehensive survey that organizes evaluation methods for LLM-based agents across five perspectives, and explicitly identifies gaps. It is included here as a reference paper, not a benchmark contribution.

### [Hierarchical Decision Abstraction](../topics/hierarchical_decision_abstraction.md)

The shared question here is not just whether the agent acts well, but at which level of abstraction those decisions should be represented and judged.

- [CoLA: Controlling Large Language Model with Latent Action](../works/cola.md): CoLA learns a compact latent action space for controlling an LLM, separating the policy's decision representation from the model's token vocabulary.

## Selected Topic Developments

[Scientific Agent Benchmarks](../topics/scientific_agents.md), [Survey](../topics/survey.md), [Hierarchical Decision Abstraction](../topics/hierarchical_decision_abstraction.md) stay at the center of this month, but the useful point is not the list itself. This month mixes new benchmarks with attempts to explain what the field is actually measuring and why those measurement choices matter.
