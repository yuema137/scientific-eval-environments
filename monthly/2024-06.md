# June 2024 Monthly Report

> **English** | [简体中文](../zh/monthly/2024-06.md)

> **Coverage:** First appearances during 2024-06

## Month at a Glance

- Six works entered the knowledge base this month, and all six are new releases first made public in June 2024 — there are no backfills.
- Five are field-grounded LLM and agent benchmarks filed under [Scientific Agent Benchmarks](../topics/scientific_agents.md): [AssertionBench](../works/assertionbench.md) and [VHDL-Eval](../works/vhdl-eval.md) in hardware design, [MatText](../works/mattext.md) in materials, and [GenoTEX](../works/genotex.md) and [BioKGBench](../works/biokgbench.md) in the life sciences. The sixth, [NATURAL PLAN](../works/natural-plan.md), sits under [Planning & Decision-Making Evaluation](../topics/planning_decision_evaluation.md).
- The recurring design choice is verification-grounded ground truth: correctness is checked against something external and objective — formally verified assertions, self-verifying testbenches, expert-curated reference pipelines, geometric-model regression baselines, or the knowledge graph under audit — rather than surface plausibility or a single canonical string.
- The recurring result is a bounded negative finding. Each benchmark reports where current models fall short and localizes the gap, rather than announcing a new capability.

## What Changed This Month

**Verification-grounded ground truth is the shared design move.** The older evaluation path for generative tasks scores an output by matching it to a reference answer or by judging plausibility, which is cheap but accepts fluent-but-wrong outputs and can reject valid variants. Five of the six June works replace that with a check that lives outside the model's output. [AssertionBench](../works/assertionbench.md) scores generated assertions against a set that formal tools (GoldMine and HARM) have already verified. [VHDL-Eval](../works/vhdl-eval.md) runs each generated design through a self-verifying testbench. [GenoTEX](../works/genotex.md) compares an agent's full analysis pipeline against reference code and results curated by bioinformaticians. [MatText](../works/mattext.md) measures property-prediction error against geometric graph-neural-network baselines on the same crystals. [BioKGBench](../works/biokgbench.md) points the check at the knowledge graph itself: success means finding a factual error the graph actually contains. The benefit is that a passing score is hard to fake; the limit is that each check inherits the coverage of its oracle — 100 designs, 202 problems, a fixed set of databases — so the score is only as broad as the curated reference behind it.

**LLM hardware evaluation extends past Verilog code generation.** Two works widen hardware-description-language evaluation in different directions. VHDL-Eval addresses the language gap: because Verilog dominates existing LLM hardware benchmarks, it assembles 202 problems (Verilog problems translated to VHDL plus aggregated public VHDL challenges), each with a self-verifying testbench, and reports that general models transfer poorly — VHDL-specific supervised fine-tuning is necessary. AssertionBench addresses the task gap: instead of generating a design, the model must generate the assertions that state what a design must never violate, scored against formally verified references over 100 OpenCores designs. Read together they mark two edges of the same expansion — from writing hardware to specifying and verifying it, and from one HDL to another — and both conclude that off-the-shelf models are not yet sufficient.

**The month's benchmarks converge on localized shortfalls rather than headline wins.** Where a score is reported, it reads as a ceiling, and the contribution is diagnosis. MatText sweeps nine text representations, model scales up to 70B parameters, and datasets up to 2M structures and still finds a "geometric blindness": text-based LLMs capture category patterns but miss coordinate information, and geometric architectures beat them by wide margins. NATURAL PLAN reports that every evaluated model falls below 5% exact match on ten-city trip instances. BioKGBench reports that state-of-the-art agents, general and biomedical alike, fail or underperform. GenoTEX's error analysis foregrounds the challenges that remain. None of these claims a solved task; each names which step breaks.

**NATURAL PLAN isolates planning from tool operation.** The usual difficulty with an agent-planning benchmark is that a failure could be tool selection, tool execution, or retrieval rather than planning itself. NATURAL PLAN removes that ambiguity by placing the outputs of Google Flights, Google Maps, and Google Calendar directly in the prompt, so its 3,600 trip, meeting, and calendar instances test plan construction under fixed information. The trade-off is explicit: with retrieval and environment adaptation removed and only single-response plans measured, the benchmark reads planning cleanly but does not speak to feedback-conditioned replanning or live tool use.

## Selected Topic Developments

### [Scientific Agent Benchmarks](../topics/scientific_agents.md)

Five of the month's six works land here, and they share a stance: take a task a domain expert normally does by hand and make it measurable against an objective reference. They differ in what the expert task is.

Two are hardware works, treated together above under the HDL expansion — [AssertionBench](../works/assertionbench.md) on formally grounded assertion generation and [VHDL-Eval](../works/vhdl-eval.md) on VHDL code generation with self-verifying testbenches — both concluding that general models do not yet clear the bar.

[MatText](../works/mattext.md) turns "how you encode a crystal as text" into a controlled variable: nine representations, scales up to 70B parameters and 2M structures, all evaluated against geometric GNN baselines. Its finding is structural rather than incremental — a "GNN-LM wall" where text encodings miss the coordinate information that geometry-aware models exploit — which makes it an analysis-and-position result as much as a benchmark.

[GenoTEX](../works/genotex.md) and [BioKGBench](../works/biokgbench.md) move the target to the life sciences and to whole workflows. GenoTEX scores an agent's entire gene-expression analysis pipeline — dataset selection, preprocessing, statistics — against reference code and results curated by bioinformaticians (1,384 problems over 911 datasets per the official repository), so grading happens step by step rather than only at the final answer. BioKGBench decomposes literature understanding into two atomic tasks (scientific claim verification and knowledge-graph question answering) and composes them into the agentic KGCheck audit, where the evaluation target is the knowledge graph itself; the paper's baseline agent surfaced over 90 real factual errors in existing biomedical databases, a rare external check on a benchmark's realism.

### [Planning & Decision-Making Evaluation](../topics/planning_decision_evaluation.md)

[NATURAL PLAN](../works/natural-plan.md) is this topic's sole June entry, and its contribution is a boundary choice: hold information access fixed so that planning ability can be read without the confound of tool selection or execution. Google Flights, Maps, and Calendar outputs are supplied in context, and the three task families vary constraint complexity through city count, participants, schedules, and days. The reported numbers — GPT-4 at 31.1% and Gemini 1.5 Pro at 34.8% exact match on Trip Planning, with every model below 5% on ten-city trips — quantify how quickly constraint growth outruns current planners. The design's cost mirrors its benefit: exact match can reject valid alternative plans, and measuring one-shot plan production says nothing about replanning under feedback.
