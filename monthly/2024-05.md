## Month at a Glance

- Four works entered the knowledge base this month, all new releases, and all filed under [Scientific Agent Benchmarks](../topics/scientific_agents.md): [AgentClinic](../works/agentclinic.md), [CPsyExam](../works/cpsyexam.md), [AnalogCoder](../works/analogcoder.md), and [RTL-Repo](../works/rtl-repo.md).
- The shared move is narrowing. Each benchmark targets one professional field — clinical medicine, psychology, analog hardware, digital hardware — rather than general capability.
- The batch splits on how much agency it actually exercises. AgentClinic and AnalogCoder put a model inside an interactive or iterative loop; CPsyExam and RTL-Repo score a single response.
- AgentClinic supplies the month's load-bearing finding, and it cuts across that split: the same medical knowledge, evaluated as sequential clinical work instead of static questions, reportedly falls to below a tenth of its static-QA accuracy — a direct caution against reading a static domain-knowledge score as a measure of what a model can do.
- Verification rigor, not domain, is the real dividing line. AgentClinic and AnalogCoder ground scores in execution — a moderated clinical environment and runnable Python circuits — while CPsyExam and RTL-Repo score against reference answers.

## What Changed This Month

**Domain-specialized agent benchmarks arrive as a cluster.** Every work added this month narrows evaluation to a single professional field and asks whether a model can do that field's work, not whether it can answer general questions. [AgentClinic](../works/agentclinic.md) targets clinical medicine across nine specialties and seven languages; [CPsyExam](../works/cpsyexam.md) targets psychology through Chinese examination questions; [AnalogCoder](../works/analogcoder.md) targets analog circuit design; [RTL-Repo](../works/rtl-repo.md) targets repository-scale digital hardware in Verilog. The value of grouping them is not that they cover one domain — they cover four — but that four independent teams reached for the same framing in the same month: general benchmarks say too little about performance inside a specialty, so build the specialty into the task.

**The batch splits on whether it exercises agency.** Two of the four keep the "agent" label honest. AgentClinic runs a doctor agent through sequential patient encounters, where it must elicit history, order measurements, and commit to a diagnosis under incomplete information; AnalogCoder runs an LLM agent through a feedback-driven self-correcting loop that generates and repairs Python code until a circuit works. The other two are single-shot despite the shared topic. CPsyExam distills a 22,000-question pool down to a balanced 4,000-question set and scores one answer per question, separating psychological-knowledge recall from case-analysis application along two axes; RTL-Repo asks for one Verilog completion per sample, conditioned on the full surrounding repository. Reading a static domain-knowledge score as evidence of agentic competence is exactly the inference the next story line warns against.

**AgentClinic measures the cost of collapsing agency into a question.** The older evaluation path for medical models scores static multiple-choice QA: the model sees a fully specified vignette and picks an answer. That path measures recall of curated facts, not the work of arriving at a diagnosis when the facts must be gathered. AgentClinic changes that step. It surrounds the doctor agent with patient, measurement, and moderator agents — a full simulated environment rather than a prompt — and reports that diagnostic accuracy can fall to below a tenth of the static-QA figure once the same questions become sequential encounters. It also carries tools that make learning measurable across cases (a persistent cross-case notebook reportedly gives Llama-3 up to a 92% relative improvement), models 24 cognitive and implicit biases as a robustness axis, and is backed by real electronic health records and a clinical reader study. The result stops at what the card's sources support: the numbers here come from the abstract and official project materials, and Claude 3.5-based agents are reported to lead most settings, but full per-setting validation is pending. The finding that generalizes beyond medicine is the order-of-magnitude gap itself — a reason to treat this month's static domain scores as knowledge probes, not capability measures.

**Hardware design appears twice, scored two different ways.** AnalogCoder and RTL-Repo both evaluate models on electrical and hardware design, and their contrast is instructive because it is about verification, not subject matter. AnalogCoder is training-free: its agent writes Python that constructs analog circuits, self-corrects against feedback, and banks solved sub-circuits in a reusable tool library; on a curated set of 24 tasks it designs 20 circuits successfully, five more than standard GPT-4o, scored by execution as Pass@1/Pass@5. RTL-Repo goes the opposite way on rigor to gain scale and realism: over 4,000 Verilog samples drawn from public GitHub projects, each completion judged against the reference by edit similarity and exact match rather than simulation, across GPT-4, GPT-3.5, Starcoder2, and Verilog-specialized models (VeriGen, RTLCoder). One executes the artifact and checks whether it works; the other checks whether the text matches and, as its own card notes, does not verify functional correctness. Within a single domain, the month already displays the spread between execution-grounded and string-match evaluation.

## Selected Topic Developments

### [Scientific Agent Benchmarks](../topics/scientific_agents.md)

This topic gains four domain-specialized entries at once, and together they mark out the design lines the topic has to distinguish. AgentClinic defines the **interactive-environment** line: the unit of evaluation is a moderated multi-agent setting, and the score reflects behavior over a trajectory — a use of the task axis that spans both [Scientific Problem Solving & Reasoning](../activities/scientific_problem_solving_reasoning.md) and [Experiment Design & Scientific Discovery](../activities/experiment_design_discovery.md). AnalogCoder sits on an adjacent **iterative-design** line, where an [Optimization & Engineering Design](../activities/optimization_engineering_design.md) agent loops through generate-and-repair until an executable artifact passes. CPsyExam holds the **static domain-QA** line, a single-response [Scientific Problem Solving & Reasoning](../activities/scientific_problem_solving_reasoning.md) probe that this topic must keep separate from the interactive ones. RTL-Repo extends the completion line into **repository scale**, a [Scientific Software & Workflow Engineering](../activities/scientific_software_workflow_engineering.md) task whose novelty is cross-file context rather than an interactive loop. The per-work explanations live in *What Changed This Month* above; the point here is that "Scientific Agent Benchmark" is not one design but at least three, and this month populates each — with AgentClinic's static-to-sequential collapse standing as the empirical argument for why the interactive lines are not interchangeable with the static one.
