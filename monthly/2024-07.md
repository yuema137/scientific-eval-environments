## Month at a Glance

- Six works entered the knowledge base this month, all new releases whose first appearance falls in July 2024. Five carry the [Scientific Agent Benchmarks](../topics/scientific_agents.md) topic; the sixth, [BadRobot](../works/badrobot.md), is a [Robotics](../domains/robotics.md) safety probe with no methodology-axis topic.
- The spine of the month is field-specific benchmarking: each work measures one science on that field's own terms, importing a domain success criterion rather than reusing a generic one.
- The sharpest signal is where the benchmark places its checkable target. When a work scores recall of field knowledge, frontier models look strong — [AstroMLab 1](../works/astromlab-1.md) tops out near 85% on astronomy multiple choice. When a work instead verifies a completed artifact, they collapse: [SciCode](../works/scicode.md) reports 4.6% and [MolPuzzle](../works/molpuzzle.md) 1.4%.
- Two works change what counts as a good answer rather than what is asked. [ElecBench](../works/elecbench.md) makes grid stability and security first-class metrics; [BadRobot](../works/badrobot.md) moves safety evaluation from what a model says to what a robot does.
- The month carries its own caveat: high recall scores are explicitly a lower bound. AstroMLab 1's items are LLM-generated with only a subset human-verified, and closed-book recall over review literature is knowledge, not the ability to carry out research.

## What Changed This Month

**Domain-specific scientific benchmarking becomes the dominant activity.** The recurring earlier pattern was to probe a science by dressing a general-knowledge test in field vocabulary. This month's five capability benchmarks each start from what their field actually demands. [AstroMLab 1](../works/astromlab-1.md) sources 4,425 questions from the *Annual Review of Astronomy and Astrophysics* so items reflect consolidated astronomy rather than trivia; [LAB-Bench](../works/lab-bench.md) organizes its eight categories around the daily verbs of biology research — read literature, interpret figures, query databases, manipulate sequences; [ElecBench](../works/elecbench.md) builds its rubric from grid-operation priorities; [MolPuzzle](../works/molpuzzle.md) follows the chemist's spectra-to-structure workflow; and [SciCode](../works/scicode.md) draws its problems from real research code across sixteen natural-science subfields. The common move is to let the domain define the task, not the other way around.

**The gap between recall and execution is the month's clearest result.** These benchmarks differ most in what they verify. AstroMLab 1 and LAB-Bench check answers against a key on multiple-choice items, and on the recall end the strongest models score high — Claude-3.5-Sonnet reaches 85.0% on AstroMLab 1. MolPuzzle and SciCode instead verify a completed object: an assembled molecular structure judged by exact match, or executable research code run against scientist-written test cases. Under that stricter oracle the best models tested fall to 1.4% (GPT-4o on MolPuzzle) and 4.6% (Claude 3.5 Sonnet on SciCode's most realistic setting). The step that changes is the checkable target, not the subject matter, and the drop points at composition — assembling correct sub-steps into a correct whole — rather than knowledge as the failure mode.

**A metric taxonomy can encode a domain's constraints.** Most LLM evaluation reduces to some blend of factual correctness. Power dispatch cannot: a grid answer that is factually right but destabilizing is a bad answer. [ElecBench](../works/elecbench.md) reflects this by promoting stability and security to first-class metrics alongside factuality, logicality, fairness, and expressiveness, then subdividing the six into 24 sub-metrics. The rubric itself, not just the question set, carries the domain's priorities.

**Safety evaluation crosses the say/do boundary.** Chat-side jailbreak work asks whether a model will *say* something harmful. [BadRobot](../works/badrobot.md) asks whether an embodied stack will *do* it, issuing 277 malicious queries across six harm categories to frameworks that emit executable action plans and closing the loop on real UR3e and myCobot arms. The verified endpoint moves from text to executed physical action, and the finding is that language-level safety training does not reliably survive translation into that action.

## Selected Topic Developments

### [Scientific Agent Benchmarks](../topics/scientific_agents.md)

Five of the month's six works land in this topic, and the useful reading is the spectrum they span rather than any single entry. They line up by how far the evaluated task sits from recall and how strict the verification is.

- **Recall end.** [AstroMLab 1](../works/astromlab-1.md) is the first astronomy-specific LLM benchmark: 4,425 four-option questions generated by Gemini-1.5-Pro from 885 review articles, scored by accuracy across 47 proprietary and open-weights models, with cost-efficiency and confidence-calibration analyses layered on top. It measures knowledge, and it measures it well — Claude-3.5-Sonnet leads the proprietary models at 85.0%, LLaMA-3-70B the open-weights at 80.6%. But the card is careful about what the number means: the items are machine-generated with only a subset human-verified, older reviews may encode outdated astronomy, and the authors call the reported accuracy "certainly a lower limit." Closed-book multiple choice with retrieval explicitly out of scope tells you what a model retains, not what it can do. It links [Scientific Problem Solving & Reasoning](../activities/scientific_problem_solving_reasoning.md).

- **Practice middle.** [LAB-Bench](../works/lab-bench.md) keeps the multiple-choice form but points it at research practice: over 2,400 questions across eight categories — literature recall and reasoning, figure and table interpretation, database access, protocol planning, and DNA/protein sequence manipulation, including the multi-step CloningScenarios — scored against human expert biologist baselines, with a held-back private split guarding against contamination. The reported model-versus-expert figures are not established from the sources on file, so the contribution here is the reframing from exam knowledge to research verbs rather than a headline score. It cross-links [Literature Search & Evidence Synthesis](../activities/literature_evidence_synthesis.md) and [Scientific Problem Solving & Reasoning](../activities/scientific_problem_solving_reasoning.md).

- **Execution end.** [MolPuzzle](../works/molpuzzle.md) and [SciCode](../works/scicode.md) swap the checkable target from an answer choice to a finished artifact. MolPuzzle stages molecular structure elucidation as chemists work it — derive constraints from the formula, interpret IR/MS/¹H-NMR/¹³C-NMR spectra, then assemble the molecule — and judges the final structure by exact match; GPT-4o performs best yet matches ground truth on only 1.4% of structures, far below humans, and the three-stage decomposition localizes where elucidation breaks down. SciCode takes 80 scientist-authored research problems factored into 338 subproblems and runs generated code against gold solutions and test cases; the main-problem/subproblem split separates composition failures from unit failures, and in the most realistic setting the best model tested, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, solves 4.6% of main problems. SciCode additionally spans [Scientific Software & Workflow Engineering](../activities/scientific_software_workflow_engineering.md); both link [Scientific Problem Solving & Reasoning](../activities/scientific_problem_solving_reasoning.md).

- **Rubric variant.** [ElecBench](../works/elecbench.md) sits apart because it changes the scoring frame rather than the verification target: eight LLMs answer general-knowledge and professional-business power-dispatch scenarios, graded on six metrics subdivided into 24 sub-metrics in which stability and security are first-class. Its domain-specific contribution is developed under [Selected Domain Developments](#selected-domain-developments) below.

The topic's boundary this month is visible in the numbers: as verification tightens from answer-key recall to artifact execution, reported frontier accuracy moves from the mid-80s to low single digits. The month does not settle whether that gap reflects a capability ceiling or benchmark difficulty calibration — it documents the gap and where each benchmark stops.

## Selected Domain Developments

### [Energy Systems](../domains/energy_systems.md)

Power dispatch is a decision domain where correctness alone is insufficient — a response can be factually accurate and still unsafe to act on. [ElecBench](../works/elecbench.md) is this month's entry, and its contribution is to encode that constraint into the evaluation rather than leave it to the reader. Where a generic LLM benchmark would score factual correctness, ElecBench promotes stability and security to first-class metrics alongside factuality, logicality, fairness, and expressiveness, and splits general knowledge from professional business so recall is separated from operational competence. The result stops where the setting stops: it is static, single-turn scenario answering over eight models, with no interactive grid environment and, per the card, a code URL not independently confirmed live.

### [Robotics](../domains/robotics.md)

[BadRobot](../works/badrobot.md) is the month's field-axis outlier — a safety and robustness probe rather than a scientific-capability benchmark, which is why it carries no methodology-axis topic and an explicit `N/A` on the activity axis. It reframes jailbreak evaluation for embodied stacks: three identified vulnerability classes structure the attack space, a paired benchmark of 277 malicious queries across six harm categories makes refusal behavior comparable across frameworks such as Code as Policies, ProgPrompt, and VoxPoser, and the attack reaches an average manipulate success rate of 68.57%, validated on real UR3e and myCobot 280-Pi arms. The step it changes is the endpoint of verification — from harmful text to executed physical action — and the boundary is that this card covers the benchmark side of a paper whose primary contribution is the attack paradigm, with query counts and the success rate taken from the paper body rather than the abstract.
