# August 2026 Monthly Report

> **English** | [简体中文](../zh/monthly/2026-08.md)

> **Coverage:** First appearances during 2026-08

## Month at a Glance

August 2026 is the densest month in the archive so far, with 42 first appearances, and it reads less like a single trend than like a field splitting into clearly differentiated subproblems. Scientific benchmarking remains the largest surface, but the real story is the coordination among four others: trajectory evaluation is becoming a serious diagnostic discipline, long-horizon benchmarking is broadening from task completion to agent improvement loops, evaluator reliability is starting to be benchmarked in its own right, and evaluation-driven post-training is moving from tuning a model to redesigning the learning algorithm.

This is also the month where the repository's "evaluation drives development" thesis becomes easiest to see in the literature itself. Several August works are not just asking whether an agent succeeds. They are asking whether the telemetry is sufficient, whether the replay is faithful, whether the judge can be trusted, whether the harness can improve itself, and whether a benchmark predicts anything beyond the benchmark.

## What Changed This Month

### [Scientific Agent Benchmarks](../topics/scientific_agents.md)

August's scientific benchmark work is no longer one thing. It breaks into three recognizable modes. One pushes benchmark construction deeper into real scientific or engineering workflows: [Agentic Self-Driving Microscopy Benchmarks](../works/agentic-microscopy-benchmarks.md), [AutoResearchEval](../works/autoresearcheval.md), [PACE-Bench](../works/pace-bench.md), [DSAgentBench](../works/dsagentbench.md), [CRAFTS](../works/crafts.md), [MMArch](../works/mmarch.md), [TCS-Bench](../works/tcs-bench.md), [EnergyBridge](../works/energybridge.md), and [BrainBench (EEG)](../works/brainbench-eeg.md) all widen the set of professional workflows being operationalized for evaluation.

A second turns scientific experiment choice into the object being measured. [Model Discovery Agent](../works/model-discovery-agent.md) uses one Bayesian discovery loop across chemistry, counterfactual physics, probabilistic programs, and electrophysiology, then rescores held-out prediction and mechanism recovery after every intervention. The useful shift is from “did the agent eventually find a model?” to “how many experiments did it need, and did the model survive interventions it never chose?” Its new stochastic NeuronBench also makes latent process noise part of that test rather than an afterthought.

The third mode is more diagnostic and sociotechnical. [Agents Catching Agents](../works/agents-catching-agents.md) asks whether committees of agents can be socially induced to adopt shortcut cues that clinicians would ignore. [Apodex Discovery](../works/apodex-discovery.md) scores discovery trajectories blind to outcome across six process dimensions. [Science Edge Evaluation (SEE)](../works/science-edge-evaluation.md), [Replica](../works/replica.md), [Fisher-R1 / P-Bench](../works/fisher-r1.md), and [onepot-Bench 0](../works/onepot-bench.md) each push on a slightly different question about realism, replicability, or benchmark design. [Position: AI Agents in Scientific Teams Should Be Studied as Human-Agent Systems](../works/ai-agents-in-scientific-teams.md) and [Co-Evolution in Agentic Systems: Toward Self-Directed Evolution Beyond Human Design](../works/co-evolution-agentic-systems-survey.md) sit outside benchmark construction narrowly defined, but they clarify why the surrounding human and organizational structure keeps showing up in the month's empirical work.

The practical reading is that August does not offer one new gold-standard benchmark. It shows the landscape fragmenting in a productive way: some evaluators care about domain workflow fidelity, some about experiment efficiency and mechanism recovery, some about discovery process or team dynamics, and some about whether the benchmark itself predicts anything outside its own test set.

### [Trajectory Evaluation](../topics/trajectory_evaluation.md)

Trajectory evaluation becomes a field in its own right this month. [AutoResearchEval](../works/autoresearcheval.md), [Beyond Final Scores](../works/beyond-final-scores.md), [SWE-RPG](../works/a-unified-issue-resolution-benchmark-for-requireme.md), [SearchAuditor](../works/searchauditor.md), [TRAJDEBUG](../works/trajdebug.md), [TelemetrySuffBench](../works/telemetrysuffbench.md), and [The Replay Gap](../works/the-replay-gap.md) are all trying to answer versions of the same question: what evidence about an agent run is sufficient to diagnose it, replay it, attribute credit, or compare it fairly?

What changes in August is that the literature starts distinguishing several subproblems that used to blur together. One line asks whether the trace is rich enough to support diagnosis at all. Another asks whether replayed or reconstructed traces preserve the original decision-making signal. A third asks whether stage-level or counterfactual analysis can separate planning quality from late execution failure. By this point, trajectory evaluation is not just "score intermediate steps." It is becoming the methodology for turning agent behavior into inspectable evidence.

### [General Long-Horizon Agent Benchmarks](../topics/long_horizon_evaluation.md)

Long-horizon work also changes character in August. [RoboGraphBench](../works/compiling-and-benchmarking-task-state-horizons-for.md) and [PATH-Bench](../works/path-bench.md) still care about the classical problem of maintaining relevant state across many steps, but [Evo-Bench](../works/evo-bench.md), [HarnessOpt-Bench](../works/harnessopt-bench.md), [R³-Bench](../works/r3-bench.md), [SkillMisevo-Bench](../works/skillmisevo-bench.md), [SWE-Bench ProMax](../works/swe-bench-promax.md), and [PACE-Bench](../works/pace-bench.md) make the scope wider. The long-horizon object is not only "finish the task"; it is increasingly "adapt the harness," "allocate resources across many decisions," or "improve a system over repeated cycles."

That is why August feels different from earlier long-horizon months. The horizon is no longer only in the task. It is also in the development loop around the task.

### [Evaluation-Driven Post-Training](../topics/evaluation_driven_post_training.md)

[AI4AI-Bench](../works/ai4ai-bench.md) moves the intervention boundary one level deeper. The agent is not merely choosing data, hyperparameters or an existing post-training recipe. It receives ten frozen research repositories and may change losses, supervision, update rules, optimizers, schedules or other code in the learning procedure. Exploration uses a cheaper proxy, but only the source patch crosses into a fresh run scored by a fixed final evaluator; the original algorithm is rerun under the same conditions.

That design produces two answers instead of one. The final metric says whether the resulting algorithm is better, while diff classification says what the agent actually changed. Across 263 classifiable patches, 141 changed only the surrounding run and 122 touched learning itself; the latter group scored higher on average, 0.226 versus 0.126. Higher reasoning effort was also associated with a larger share of learning-side changes, although the comparison was not randomized. The useful lesson is not that one harness has solved autonomous algorithm discovery—the best system averaged 0.250 on a scale where the rerun baseline is 0.1—but that an evaluation loop can now expose whether an apparent gain came from algorithmic invention or from tuning around it.
