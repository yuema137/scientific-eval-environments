# October 2025 Monthly Report

> **English** | [简体中文](../zh/monthly/2025-10.md)

> **Coverage:** First appearances during 2025-10

## Month at a Glance

October 2025 is a second breakout month, but this time the added breadth comes with more explicit diagnostic pressure. Scientific benchmarks still dominate, yet CUARewardBench, Hard2Verify, TempoBench, Enconda-bench, and PTA-GRPO make step-level judgment, verifier quality, and high-level planning hard to separate from the benchmark story.

## What Changed This Month

### [Scientific Agent Benchmarks](../topics/scientific_agents.md)

This group asks what counts as a defensible scientific benchmark once a field's artifacts, tools, and verification constraints stop looking like generic QA.

- [A Lightweight Large Language Model-Based Multi-Agent System for 2D Frame Structural Analysis](../works/a-lightweight-large-language-model-based-multi-age.md): A benchmark of twenty 2D building-frame structural-analysis problems, each evaluated over ten independent trials, together with a five-agent Llama-3.3 70B pipeline that converts a textual frame description into executable OpenSeesPy finite-element code.
- [AFMBench](../works/afmbench.md): AFMBench is an evaluation suite of 100 expertly curated tasks that requires LLM agents to operate a real atomic force microscope rather than a simulator, spanning the workflow from experimental design through results analysis. It is documented here for AFMBench, the paper's *evaluation* contribution; the accompanying AILA framework is agent-implementation work adjacent to this repository's evaluation focus (see the repository note under Limitations).
- [An LLM-driven framework for cosmological model-building and exploration](../works/an-llm-driven-framework-for-cosmological-model-bui.md): A multi-stage evaluation framework that measures whether an LLM-driven coding agent can autonomously propose, implement and explore alternative cosmological models by modifying the CLASS Boltzmann-solver codebase inside a containerized environment. Agent submissions are scored automatically against binary execution milestones, numerical agreement with ground-truth reference implementations, and cosmological likelihood/tension rewards computed from real survey data.
- [AstaBench](../works/astabench.md): AstaBench is a benchmark suite that provides a holistic measure of agentic ability to perform scientific research, pairing its tasks with a reproducible tool environment and cost-controlled scoring. It comprises 2,400+ problems across 11 benchmarks in four task categories — literature understanding, code & execution, data analysis, and end-to-end discovery — with many problems inspired by actual user requests to deployed Asta agents.
- [AtomWorld](../works/atomworld.md): AtomWorld is a benchmark for evaluating the spatial reasoning of large language models on crystalline materials: models perform ten fundamental atomic-structure actions across four widely used modelling categories, with verifiable checks — and struggle badly on operations involving complex spatial relations (below 12% success for rotations).

### [Credit Assignment](../topics/credit_assignment.md)

The common move here is to ask which interior step deserves blame or credit, instead of letting a final reward smear across the whole run.

- [CUARewardBench](../works/cuarewardbench.md): CUARewardBench is a benchmark that treats outcome reward models (ORM) and process reward models (PRM) as the judged subjects, testing whether vision-language models can reproduce expert verdicts on computer-using agent trajectories. It pairs 272 trajectory-success annotations with 346 step-correctness annotations over OSWorld rollouts from seven policy models spanning 25.9–50.8% success rates.
- [Hard2Verify](../works/hard2verify.md): Hard2Verify is a benchmark that scores step-level verifiers on their ability to grade the individual steps of frontier-model proofs to recent, open-ended Olympiad mathematics problems. It comprises 1,860 expert-labeled steps across 200 model-generated solutions to 80 competition problems, produced with over 500 hours of human labor.
- [TempoBench](../works/tempobench.md): A formally verifiable temporal benchmark that isolates *counterfactual causal attribution* over execution trajectories, testing whether LLMs can identify which inputs were necessary for an observed output rather than merely simulating a system forward.

### [Trajectory Evaluation](../topics/trajectory_evaluation.md)

Here the unit of judgment is no longer just the final answer. The trajectory itself becomes evidence about what the agent saw, decided, and missed.

- [Enconda-bench](../works/enconda-bench.md): Enconda-bench (Environment Configuration Diagnosis Benchmark) evaluates software-engineering agents at the process level on environment configuration — a bottleneck where end-to-end build/test benchmarks obscure where and why agents fail.
- [TempoBench](../works/tempobench.md): A formally verifiable temporal benchmark that isolates *counterfactual causal attribution* over execution trajectories, testing whether LLMs can identify which inputs were necessary for an observed output rather than merely simulating a system forward.

## Selected Topic Developments

[Scientific Agent Benchmarks](../topics/scientific_agents.md), [Credit Assignment](../topics/credit_assignment.md), [Trajectory Evaluation](../topics/trajectory_evaluation.md) stay at the center of this month, but the useful point is not the list itself. These topics converge on the claim that final success is not enough evidence; several works start localizing responsibility to specific steps, verifiers, or decisions.
