# October 2026 Monthly Report

## Month at a Glance

October's additions keep pulling apart what a single success number used to merge: the derivation behind a correct formula, the stage of a workflow behind an overall score, the cost of an experiment behind a recommendation, and the evidence behind a verifier's verdict. Several of the new cards report the cases where these parts disagree, such as a correct answer whose shown derivation does not support it, or a robot control that chooses the right weight and then lets it slip.

Fifteen cards are counted for October. Thirteen first appeared this month; [DISCERN](../works/discern.md) (first public 2026-09-27) and [EurekaBench](../works/eurekabench.md) (first public 2026-09-30) are backfills. The seven cards added on 2026-10-27 appear in the complete index below; the narrative sections have not yet been regenerated to discuss them. This report was prepared on 2026-10-27, before the month closed, so any card that reaches `main` later in October is not yet included; the month's scheduled run refreshes this same file in place.

## What Changed This Month

### A correct final formula is not the whole answer

When a derivation is scored only by its final expression, a model can earn full credit without showing a valid route to it. Two theoretical-physics cards score the route as well, in different ways.

[PHYRD](../works/phyrd.md) splits each of its 106 problems into public subquestions, and a subquestion scores 1 only when the requested conclusions, the necessary derivation steps, and the applicability conditions are all correct. Its Reproduce problems rebuild a published result from stated premises; its Discover problems change one condition from a source paper and ask what follows. Scoring by part shows how answers fall short: across five models, Reproduce whole-problem accuracy ranges from 49.21% to 68.25% while subquestion accuracy ranges from 79.66% to 88.33%, and 78 of 133 incomplete Reproduce responses miss a single subquestion. On Discover, adding tools cuts calculation errors from 123 to 67 across 209 paired answers, while conceptual errors move from 70 to 75. The paper itself notes that the two Discover settings differ in more than tool access, and it does not say whether humans, a model judge, or symbolic checks apply its scoring rules.

[QFT-Bench](../works/qft-bench.md) keeps a single closed-form target for each of its 75 quantum field theory problems and settles correctness symbolically first, calling an LLM judge only when SymPy is inconclusive. A separate judge then asks whether the displayed derivation supports the correct answer. Across 39 models, twelve exceed 95% accuracy, yet on average 7.2% of correct answers are labeled unfaithful, and Llama models reach roughly 20–40%. The faithfulness measure is also the weaker instrument: on 60 expert-reviewed responses, judge agreement is 98% and 92% for correctness but 73% and 67% for faithfulness.

### One overall score hides which stage failed

[DISCERN](../works/discern.md) (backfill) scores data vetting, analysis verification, and hypothesis generation as separate levels on eight life-science tracks, and combines them with a geometric mean so that a weak level lowers the track composite. Its matched clean controls make over-rejection visible: only 19 of 64 Level 1 clean-control evaluations correctly accept the data. Perfect scores fall from 60.8% of Level 1 evaluations to 34.2% at Level 2 and 0.6% at Level 3. The tasks are bounded, the levels are instantiated independently, and Level 3 rests on an LLM judge plus author validation, so the card does not establish autonomous end-to-end research.

[Labference](../works/labference.md) applies the same separation to a laboratory robot: operation selection, physical execution, and task completion are scored apart, because a correct choice can be lost to a dropped object and a successful transfer can carry the wrong object. The first results show the two failure routes in different systems. In simulated easy feedback weighing, fine-tuned π0.5 and GR00T-N1.5 select the required standard in 20% and 18% of 50 episodes each, while a GPT-driven control selects correctly in 10 of 10 trials but completes 8, with failures from weights slipping or falling and about 30 minutes of model computation per trial. Only this easy weighing task has quantitative results so far; the other families and the planned diagnostics are stated as future work.

### The budget becomes part of the task

These two cards put a budget inside the task, but they budget different things.

In [SlowLab](../works/slowlab.md), the budget constrains the agent's own experiments. Over a simulated one-year greenhouse campaign, each crop occupies one of four compartments for 180 days and can only be continued or destroyed, and the recommended climate policy is scored privately in weather years the agent never saw. In a preregistered study on 48 unseen sites, three low-cost models end near a fixed grower setting (full-feedback means of 28.02–29.62 against 29.34 EUR/m²) and below a prior-informed Bayesian-optimisation baseline (33.62). In 94–100% of Full-feedback branches, the agents plant their second wave only after the first closes, so in-season readings never change what they plant. The result describes the simulation: the simulator failed the paper's external check for air temperature and humidity, and the baseline carries domain priors the models lack.

In [AlphaIdeaBench](../works/alphaideabench.md), the budget constrains the research a proposal describes, not the ideation run. Each of 100 machine-learning tasks states GPUs, API credit, calendar time, and annotation hours, and an LLM judge checks whether the proposed plan fits them among twelve scored dimensions. Running eight harnesses on one backbone and eight backbones inside one harness separates harness effects from model effects: harness scores span 4.02 to 4.31, while swapping CoI-Agent's backbone moves its score from 4.31 to 1.84. Proposals are never executed, so feasibility here is a judgment of a written plan.

### Where a retrieval gain comes from

[Astro2Method](../works/astro2method.md) asks a model to choose the better machine-learning method for an astronomy problem, with the answer key taken from the source paper's significance-filtered comparison. Difficulty depends on how fully the source explains the result: under direct prompting, models score 87.50–92.55% on Easy items and 58.87–64.52% on Hard ones. ReAct, Self-Ask, and CRITIC, which search only a historical corpus of 2014–2023 papers, score 1.4–6.2 points below direct prompting overall. Required web search lifts Hard accuracy to 70.97–75.00%, but the gain comes mainly from questions where search found the source paper itself, while supplying the masked source pipeline description raises Hard accuracy by 12.1–14.5 points. A label records which method won under the source paper's own setup, not that method's advantage elsewhere, and the authors cannot rule out that source papers appear in training data.

### A verifier written once, tested after the task shifts

[AgentProofBench](../works/agentproofbench.md) moves outside science to Web-service agents. A model writes an acceptance program once; the program is frozen and then judges terminal states from starting conditions that change what the same instruction requires. Reused programs stay close to rebuilt ones: related-state balanced accuracy is 93.23% and 90.36% with DeepSeek Flash and Pro as generators, and the reuse-minus-rebuild intervals are [−5.47, 1.57] and [−10.16, 0.00] points. On the natural panel, fresh online judgments are more accurate (99.0% against 92.1% balanced accuracy), while a program's construction cost breaks even against fresh online judging after a median of 12 or 10 assessments. The card also links panel correctness to delivery: 32 of 33 assignments with error-free related panels deliver both openings correctly, against 1 of 11 with panel errors. That link is exploratory, resting on 44 assignments from 22 families, and all generator, judge, and actor results use those two DeepSeek models.

## Selected Topic Developments

[Evaluator Reliability & Validation](../topics/evaluator_reliability_validation.md) gains three cards that test a judge against three different references. AgentProofBench checks a frozen program against changed starting states and against what an agent finally delivers. AlphaIdeaBench checks its novelty judge against scores mapped from peer-review comments on 600 papers; within-±1 agreement is about 94.5–97.4% for problem novelty but about 80.6–87.5% for scenario novelty, and the reference mapping was done by the same model as the production judge. QFT-Bench compares its correctness and faithfulness judges with three QFT experts. AlphaIdeaBench and QFT-Bench both report agreement per axis, and it is lowest on scenario novelty and on faithfulness respectively.

[Resource-aware Evaluation](../topics/resource_aware_evaluation.md) groups three cards whose resources are not interchangeable. AlphaIdeaBench budgets the proposed research. DISCERN reports tokens, API calls, and estimated cost beside each score, excluding grading, and treats its dollar figures as upper bounds. SlowLab budgets experiments, decision calls, and prompt characters, and its formal run of 480 campaigns cost USD 18.70 in API spend. Reading a cost column therefore requires checking which of these it counts.

[Benchmark Design, Validity & Contamination](../topics/benchmark_design_validity_contamination.md) receives PHYRD and SlowLab, both of which make their protocol auditable while stating a limit. PHYRD plans timestamped additions with hash-versioned task packages, and its authors note that models may have seen related published results. SlowLab hash-locked its sample size, tests, models, prompts, code, and configuration before the formal run, and reports that its simulator failed part of its own external check.

Labference also enters [Planning & Decision-Making Evaluation](../topics/planning_decision_evaluation.md) through its next-operation choice, and Astro2Method enters [Agent Harnesses & Scaffolding](../topics/agent_harnesses_scaffolding.md) through its scaffold comparison.

## Selected Domain Developments

[Physics](../domains/physics.md) receives both derivation benchmarks, and both lean on high-energy theory. QFT-Bench is scoped to quantum field theory and is presented as Part I of a broader high-energy evaluation; in PHYRD, high-energy physics supplies 39 of 63 Reproduce and 20 of 43 Discover problems. The month's other domain additions are single cards in [Astronomy](../domains/astronomy.md), [Biology](../domains/biology.md), [Robotics](../domains/robotics.md), and [AI & Machine Learning Research](../domains/ai_ml_research.md).
