# NewtonBench (2025)

> **English** | [简体中文](../zh/works/newtonbench.md)

## Overview

NewtonBench is a benchmark that evaluates scientific law discovery in LLM agents through interactive experimentation on simulated physical systems. It comprises 324 tasks generated by applying counterfactual law shifts to 12 canonical physics laws.

## Summary

NewtonBench argues that existing law-discovery benchmarks face a methodological trilemma among scientific relevance, scalability, and memorization resistance, and that they oversimplify discovery as static function fitting. It instead has agents uncover laws embedded in simulated model systems by iteratively running experiments, across 324 tasks derived from counterfactual shifts of canonical physics laws. Experiments on frontier LLMs reveal a clear but fragile discovery capability: GPT-5 reaches 72.9% overall symbolic accuracy, but performance degrades sharply with system complexity and observational noise, and a code interpreter can hurt more capable models by inducing a premature shift from exploration to exploitation.

## Tasks

324 tasks: 108 shifted laws, each instantiated in three model systems (Vanilla Equation, Simple System, Complex System). Shifted laws are curated from 12 canonical laws by mutating the equation's expression tree — altering operators or numeric constants — with Easy, Medium, and Hard tiers defined by cumulative mutation count (each tier 1–2 mutations beyond the previous). In every task the agent proposes input assignments to a `run_experiment` tool, observes the model's outputs, and must submit the hidden target law as a symbolic expression.

## Domains

Twelve physics domains, one canonical law each: Newton's law of universal gravitation, Coulomb's law, Ampere's force law, Fourier's law, Snell's law, radioactive decay, damped harmonic motion, Malus's law, the speed of sound in an ideal gas, Hooke's law, the Bose-Einstein distribution, and heat transfer.

## Evaluation

- **Symbolic accuracy.** Binary equivalence between the submitted expression and the ground-truth law, checked by an LLM-as-a-judge protocol that disregards physical constant values; the paper reports 98.3% agreement with human experts.
- **RMSLE.** Root mean squared logarithmic error between the discovered equation's predictions and ground-truth outputs, complementing the binary check with a continuous data-fidelity score.
- **Reported.** Across 11 LLMs in two configurations, averaged over four runs: GPT-5 leads with 72.9% symbolic accuracy as a Vanilla Agent and Gemini-2.5-pro reaches 65.0%; with a code interpreter the two fall to 69.6% and 62.0%. Accuracy degrades sharply from the Vanilla Equation setting to the Complex System setting, and Gaussian noise of just 0.0001 costs 13–15% accuracy (measured on GPT-5-mini).

## Typical Duration

Multi-turn interactive discovery: agents iteratively invoke `run_experiment` to build their own observation set before submitting a law. The paper caps each task at up to 10 experimental rounds with at most 20 input-parameter sets per round; results are reported as means over four runs.

## Main Contribution

A scalable, memorization-resistant benchmark that reframes scientific law discovery as interactive model discovery, requiring agents to experimentally probe simulated systems governed by counterfactually shifted physics laws.

## Key Design Ideas

- Counterfactual law shifts: expression-tree mutations that alter operators or numeric constants, generating plausible variants designed to resist solving by recall of known laws.
- Interactive model discovery rather than static function fitting: agents choose experiment inputs via a `run_experiment` tool and observe outputs, building their own dataset of input–output pairs.
- Three model-system settings — Vanilla Equation, Simple System, Complex System — that embed the same target law under increasing confounding from surrounding structure.
- Two agent configurations, a Vanilla Agent and an Agent with Code Assistance holding a Python interpreter, isolating the effect of tool access on discovery behavior.

## Strengths

- Task generation by mutation decouples discovery from recall: a model that recalls the textbook law must still detect how the shifted law differs.
- The mutation generator scales the suite without new expert annotation while keeping laws anchored to scientifically plausible forms.
- Difficulty, system complexity, noise, and tool access vary independently, exposing the failure modes the paper documents: degradation with complexity, extreme noise sensitivity, and tool-induced satisficing.

## Limitations

- Repository note: Discovery is scored against a single closed-form target equation whose ground truth is a mutated textbook law, and the LLM-judged equivalence check deliberately disregards physical constant values, so constant calibration goes unassessed.
- Repository note: The noise-robustness finding is measured on a single model (GPT-5-mini) at four Gaussian noise levels, so per-model robustness profiles for the frontier models are not reported.
