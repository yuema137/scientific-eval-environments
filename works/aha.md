# AHA (2024)

> **English** | [简体中文](../zh/works/aha.md)

## Overview

AHA is an open-source vision-language model for detecting and reasoning over failures in robotic manipulation, framing failure detection as free-form natural-language reasoning; it is trained on the AHA dataset — a large-scale robotic failure-trajectory collection generated by procedurally perturbing successful simulation demonstrations with the FailGen framework — and surpasses GPT-4o in-context learning by 10.3%.

## Summary

FailGen turns successful RLBench demonstrations into failures by procedural perturbation — grasp slips, translation and rotation offsets, wrong sequencing — yielding the AHA dataset over 79 manipulation tasks (official repository; the project page says 50+, a discrepancy between official sources). AHA, fine-tuned on this data, detects failures and explains them in free-form language, generalizing to real-world failure datasets and unseen tasks: +10.3% over GPT-4o in-context learning, +35.3% over the average of six compared models. Plugged into downstream pipelines (reward refinement, task-and-motion planning, sub-task verification), it lifts task success by 21.4% on average versus GPT-4 models. Evaluation splits (AHA test, ManiSkill FailGen data, RoboFail) are released with three metrics.

## Tasks

Failure detection and free-form failure reasoning over robotic manipulation trajectories; simulation-generated training data with evaluation on real-world failure datasets and unseen tasks. Static per-trajectory reasoning, with agentic use via downstream integrations.

## Domains

Robotics — robotic-manipulation failure detection across simulated (RLBench, ManiSkill) and real-world failure data, deployed as a critic inside robot task pipelines.

## Evaluation

- Released evaluation splits scored by LLM fuzzy semantic similarity, ROUGE-L, and binary success (repository metric terms).
- **Reported.** +10.3% over the second-best model (GPT-4o ICL); +35.3% over the six-model average; +21.4% average downstream task-success gain across three integrations.

## Typical Duration

Per-trajectory failure analysis; downstream integrations operate inside longer robot task loops.

## Main Contribution

Showing failure understanding is trainable at scale: procedural perturbation manufactures the failure data that real robots rarely record, and a modest fine-tuned VLM beats frontier models at explaining what went wrong.

## Key Design Ideas

- Perturbing successes into failures gives dense, labeled failure data for free.
- Free-form reasoning (not classification) makes the output directly usable by planners and reward generators.
- Downstream integrations measure failure understanding by its utility, not just its accuracy.

## Strengths

- The failure-data-at-scale recipe transfers to any simulator.
- Cross-domain generalization from simulated training to real-world failure datasets is demonstrated, not assumed.
