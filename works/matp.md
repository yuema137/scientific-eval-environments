# MATP (2025)

> **English** | [简体中文](../zh/works/matp.md)

## Overview

MATP (Multi-step Automatic Theorem Proving) is an evaluation framework that verifies LLM-generated reasoning by translating each natural-language step into First-Order Logic and adjudicating it with an automated theorem prover. It is applied to a benchmark of 10,830 reasoning instances produced by 10 LLMs on tasks drawn from PrOntoQA-OOD, ProofWriter, and FOLIO.

## Summary

MATP addresses the limitation that fluent language can mask subtle logical errors in multi-step LLM reasoning, which fact-checking, self-consistency, and rule-based validation detect only partially. The framework translates premises, the candidate conclusion, and every generated reasoning step into one unified First-Order Logic representation, converts the formulas to TPTP, and applies an automated theorem prover both to label each step and to search for a valid proof path supporting the conclusion. Across 10,830 reasoning instances generated by 10 LLMs, the authors report that MATP surpasses prompting-based baselines by more than 42 percentage points in reasoning step verification, and that reasoning models produce more logically coherent chains than general models despite similar answer accuracy.

## Tasks

1,083 test cases sampled from three deductive-reasoning datasets — 384 from PrOntoQA-OOD, 344 from ProofWriter, and 355 from FOLIO — each supplying premises, a candidate conclusion, and a ground-truth label. Sampling deliberately targets harder subsets: the 4-hop subset of PrOntoQA-OOD, the 5-hop ProofWriter test set with Unknown-label tasks excluded, and FOLIO training cases restricted to True / False with conclusions that trivially repeat a premise discarded. Each case is then answered by 10 LLMs — five general and five reasoning models — yielding 10,830 benchmark instances of premises, conclusion, reasoning chain, and predicted answer.

## Domains

Deductive logical reasoning over natural-language premise sets, spanning synthetic rule-based ontologies (PrOntoQA-OOD, ProofWriter) and human-authored real-world statements about people, locations, quantities, and events (FOLIO).

## Evaluation

- **Step correctness annotation.** The prover is invoked on both a reasoning step and its negation against the premises, labelling the step True, False, or Unknown; if both are provable the premises are contradictory and Error is returned.
- **Valid proof path existence.** True-labelled steps that introduce facts not derivable from already-included steps are assembled into a candidate proof path, which is valid if the conclusion evaluated under it matches the ground-truth label.
- **Fine-grained chain classification.** Six categories combine answer correctness, step correctness, and proof-path validity — T1–T4 for correct answers and F1–F2 for incorrect ones — with Error reserved for inputs that exhaust the NL2FOL retry limit.
- **NL2FOL translation quality.** Execution Rate and Execution Accuracy at the overall level, FOL BLEU and Logical Equivalence at the sentence level.
- **Reported (Table 1).** Averaged over the 10 target models, macro F1 for reasoning step correctness is 94.26% on PrOntoQA-OOD, 91.24% on ProofWriter, and 84.42% on FOLIO, against GPT-4o prompting baselines at 47.79%, 49.36%, and 45.17% and DeepSeek-R1 baselines at 61.30%, 42.43%, and 37.55%; Execution Accuracy averages 99.90%, 99.24%, and 70.88%. On a separate classification set of 200 mutated PrOntoQA-OOD tasks, MATP reaches 100% on T1–T3 and 96% on T4.

## Typical Duration

Average processing time is ~9 seconds per task for MATP, against ~6 seconds for the GPT-4o prompting baseline and 96 seconds for DeepSeek-R1. NL2FOL translation is capped at three generation attempts per input; no separate wall-clock or token budget per instance is given.

## Main Contribution

A post-hoc evaluation framework that autoformalizes premises, conclusion, and each reasoning step into First-Order Logic for adjudication by an automated theorem prover, paired with a six-category classification of reasoning chains that separates genuine deductive reasoning from information regurgitation or coincidental correctness.

## Key Design Ideas

- Single-pass NL2FOL translation of premises, candidate conclusion, and reasoning steps into one unified First-Order Logic representation, preceded by filtering of uncertain and speculative statements.
- Deterministic one-to-one FOL-to-TPTP conversion by Python script, adopted because TPTP's more complex and verbose syntax makes direct natural-language-to-TPTP translation more error-prone.
- Three-valued step verification returning True, False, or Unknown instead of a binary judgement, with regeneration triggered when the prover hits an execution error or contradicts the ground-truth label.
- Proof-path construction restricted to True-labelled steps that add new facts, separating chains that entail the conclusion from chains that merely arrive at it.

## Strengths

- Symbolic adjudication by a theorem prover makes step-level verdicts deterministic and independent of a judge model's own reasoning quality.
- Jointly considering answer correctness, step correctness, and proof-path soundness distinguishes coincidentally correct answers from genuinely sound derivations.
- Reporting translation quality alongside verification results keeps the framework's own dominant failure mode visible rather than folding it into the headline scores.

## Limitations

- Repository note: Verification quality is bounded by the NL2FOL stage, which is itself an LLM translation performed by GPT-4o — analysis failures average 1.04% on PrOntoQA-OOD and 1.22% on ProofWriter but rise to 31.92% on FOLIO, the dataset closest to everyday language.
- Repository note: Scope is confined to deductive reasoning over supplied premises, with inductive and abductive reasoning outside the paper's focus, and the judged trajectory is a text-only reasoning chain rather than a sequence of tool calls or environment actions.
