# AI's Capability in Assisting Scientific Research in Physics, Astrophysics, and Cosmology I: Literature Review (2026)

> **English** | [简体中文](../zh/works/ai-assisting-research-i-literature-review.md)

## Overview

A controlled head-to-head comparison of AI-assisted and human-expert literature review on eight real frontier research projects in physics, astrophysics and cosmology, measuring how far AI-selected references overlap with expert-selected ones and quantifying fabrication and metadata-mismatch rates across 701 AI-generated references.

## Summary

Eight frontier research projects were designed by working researchers, each accompanied by a title, background and goal of at most three sentences. For every project, one human expert — a graduate student or postdoc active on that topic — performed an independent literature search, while separate non-expert student "prompters" issued standardized prompts to three AI systems and collected their reference lists. Both sides were capped at 50 papers and required to sort them into four categories: recent review papers, historically important highly cited papers, recent papers, and other relevant papers. The domain expert then judged the relevance of every AI-returned reference, and every AI reference was verified by sequential DOI resolution, link resolution and title search. The two headline findings are that human–AI overlap is under 6% on every model, and that only a third of AI-produced references are metadata-perfect.

## Tasks

Eight expert-designed projects: AGN duty cycle with MaNGA (AGN and galaxy evolution); the galaxy–dark-matter-halo connection of Lyman-break galaxies (large-scale structure); intrinsic alignments in varying environments (weak lensing); debris emergence on laser-ablated sub-wavelength shapes (instrumentation and laser fabrication); radio galaxies with HalfDome (CMB foregrounds); the environment of gravitational-wave black-hole binaries; pulsar-timing-array sensitivity to deviations from general relativity; and massive Yang–Mills theory (theoretical cosmology and inflation). Each project generated one human reference list and one list per AI system, capped at 50 papers, with each reference required to state title, author, year, journal, DOI and link. Human experts produced 194 references in total; the AI systems produced 701.

## Domains

Astronomy and astrophysics is the dominant field: six of the eight projects are astrophysical or cosmological (AGN and galaxy evolution, Lyman-break galaxies, weak-lensing intrinsic alignments, CMB foreground radio galaxies, gravitational-wave binary environments, and pulsar timing arrays). Physics is a genuine co-domain — the pulsar-timing project tests deviations from general relativity, the massive Yang–Mills project is theoretical field theory, and the laser-ablation project is instrumentation and materials physics rather than astronomy.

## Evaluation

Two independent measurements, both grounded in the expert's judgment rather than an automatic proxy.

*Reference overlap.* A reference counts as overlapping only if it matches the human list in both title and assigned category. Every AI reference falls into exactly one of four classes: found by both human and AI; found by the human but missed by the AI; found by the AI and judged relevant by the expert but missed by the human; or found by the AI and judged irrelevant. Relevance is an explicitly subjective expert judgment, which the authors flag.

*Hallucination.* Each of the 641 AI references not also found by a human was checked by resolving its DOI, then its link, then by title search, and classified as perfect, metadata mismatch (a real paper with at least one wrong field among title, first author, year, journal, DOI or link) or fabrication (a nonexistent paper).

- **Reported (overlap, share of the combined human+AI set):** ChatGPT Deep Research 18 papers / 5.4%; ChatGPT-4o 17 papers / 4.3%; Gemini 11 papers / 2.2% — all below 6%.
- **Reported (hallucination, 641 references):** 211 perfect (33%), 408 metadata mismatch (64%), 22 fabrication (3%). Per model — ChatGPT-4o 23% perfect, 76% mismatch, 0.5% fabrication; ChatGPT Deep Research 78% perfect, 22% mismatch, 0% fabrication; Gemini 19% perfect, 75% mismatch, 7% fabrication.
- **Reported (field-level, 399 resolvable references):** first author wrong in 306 cases and title wrong in 181; about 206 references had two mismatched fields.
- **Reported (single-project probe):** every reference generated by ChatGPT Pro 5.5 on the SU(2) project was perfect, with no fabrication or metadata mismatch.

## Main Contribution

A controlled, expert-anchored measurement of AI literature review in physics, astrophysics and cosmology, showing both that AI reference selection barely overlaps with what domain experts choose and that reference *metadata* fidelity — not outright fabrication — is the dominant failure mode, at 64% of checked references.

## Key Design Ideas

- Run humans and AI on the same eight real, frontier-level projects from an identical short brief, so the comparison is like-for-like.
- Separate roles deliberately: domain experts do the human search and the relevance judging, while non-expert students issue the AI prompts, preventing expert knowledge from leaking into the AI condition.
- Require both sides to categorize references (review / highly cited / recent / other), so overlap is measured on placement as well as identity.
- Define hallucination to include metadata mismatch, not only fabrication, and verify by a fixed DOI → link → title-search cascade.
- Break mismatches down by field (title, first author, year, journal, DOI, link) to locate exactly where reference generation degrades.
- Include a tool-augmented deep-research mode alongside plain chat models, isolating the effect of genuine online search.
- Add a single-project probe with a much newer model to show the trend over model generations.

## Strengths

- The human baseline is produced by researchers actually working on each project, not by proxy annotators.
- The hallucination taxonomy is more informative than a single fabrication rate and exposes a failure mode (wrong first author in 306 of 399 resolvable references) that abstract-level checks miss.
- Verification is mechanical and reproducible (DOI and link resolution), even though relevance judgment is not.
- Covers both plain chat models and a tool-augmented deep-research mode, and the gap between them is large and interpretable.

## Limitations

- Only eight projects, one human expert per project; the authors state a more comprehensive study would need a larger set.
- Relevance judgments are explicitly subjective and made by a single expert per project, with no inter-rater agreement reported.
- The ChatGPT Pro 5.5 comparison covers one project only, so its perfect score is not a like-for-like result.
- Models are a mid-2025 snapshot and the authors present the results as a point-in-time picture.
- No dataset, prompts or verification code are stated to be released in the primary source.
- Repository note: this is an evaluation study with a fixed protocol rather than a reusable named benchmark; the protocol is described in enough detail to be re-run, but no artifact is distributed.
