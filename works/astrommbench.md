## Overview

AstroMMBench is a multimodal astronomy benchmark of 621 expert-reviewed multiple-choice questions over figures drawn from astronomy papers, spanning six astrophysical subfields, used to evaluate 25 multimodal large language models on astronomical image understanding.

## Summary

AstroMMBench targets a gap the authors identify between general multimodal benchmarks and the specific demands of astronomical figures: plots, spectra, sky images and instrument diagrams whose interpretation requires domain knowledge rather than generic visual description. Questions are generated from image–text pairs mined from arXiv astronomy TeX sources, filtered so that answering genuinely requires the image, and then reviewed by a panel of 15 astronomy experts. The resulting 621-question set is scored as plain multiple-choice accuracy across 25 open- and closed-source MLLMs, with per-subfield breakdowns showing cosmology and high-energy astrophysics to be the hardest categories.

## Tasks

621 four-option multiple-choice questions over astronomical images, distributed across six astrophysical subfields: Astrophysics of Galaxies (97), Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (111), Earth and Planetary Astrophysics (105), High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (110), Instrumentation and Methods for Astrophysics (87), and Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (111). Construction started from 19,299 image–text pairs extracted from 3,592 arXiv astronomy TeX sources covering January–July 2024; captions and in-text references were refined by LLaMA3.3-70B and candidate questions generated by InternVL2.5-78B, after which five language models filtered for items that genuinely require visual understanding. A panel of 15 astronomy experts, each holding at least a master's degree in astronomy or a related field, reviewed 1,800 randomly sampled candidates within their own subfield for image–question alignment, answer accuracy and domain-knowledge necessity, retaining the final 621 items.

## Domains

Astronomy — the entire benchmark is astronomical image interpretation, organized by the arXiv astro-ph subfield taxonomy (galaxies, cosmology, planetary, high-energy, instrumentation, solar and stellar). No non-astronomy content is included; the co-domain touching AI & Machine Learning Research is methodological only, since the object of measurement is multimodal model capability rather than an astronomical result.

## Evaluation

- Single-turn multiple-choice accuracy, reported overall and per subfield.
- Evaluation runs through VLMEvalKit; DeepSeek-V3 parses each model response to identify the intended option (A, B, C or D) before scoring.
- **Reported.** 25 MLLMs evaluated (22 open-source, 3 closed-source). Best overall accuracy: Ovis2-34B at 70.53%, ahead of ChatGPT-4o at 69.07% and Doubao-1.5-vision-pro at 68.12%. Cosmology and high-energy astrophysics are the weakest subfields; instrumentation and solar astrophysics the strongest.

## Typical Duration

Single-turn question answering with no interactive or multi-step component. The paper reports no per-task token or wall-clock budget; the full model sweep was run on hardware with eight NVIDIA A100 GPUs.

## Main Contribution

Presented by the authors as the first comprehensive benchmark for evaluating multimodal large language models on astronomical image understanding, built on paper-derived figures and validated by a 15-expert review panel.

## Key Design Ideas

- Questions are mined from real astronomy paper figures and their captions rather than authored from scratch, so the visual material matches what researchers actually read.
- A model-based filter removes items answerable from text alone, enforcing that the image carries the discriminating information.
- Expert review is subfield-matched: each reviewer judges only items in their own specialty.
- Subfield partitioning follows the arXiv astro-ph categories, making per-domain difficulty directly comparable.

## Strengths

- Broad model coverage (25 MLLMs across open and closed families) with a standardized harness (VLMEvalKit), so scores are comparable.
- Expert filtering from 1,800 candidates down to 621 is an explicit, stated quality protocol rather than an unaudited automatic pipeline.
- Per-subfield reporting localizes where multimodal models fail rather than giving a single aggregate number.

## Limitations

- Multiple-choice format only; no free-form reasoning, no tool use, and no agentic or multi-step evaluation.
- Question generation is model-authored (InternVL2.5-78B) with human review as a filter, so item phrasing inherits generator conventions.
