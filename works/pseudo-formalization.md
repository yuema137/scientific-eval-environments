## Overview

Pseudo-Formalization (PF) is an evaluation methodology that verifies natural-language mathematical proofs by rewriting them into self-contained modules — each stating its premises, conclusion, and proof — and then checking every module independently. It is evaluated on olympiad-level and research-level mathematics, and ships ArxivMathGradingBench, 35 arXiv research papers carrying 40 errors their authors later disclosed.

## Summary

Pseudo-Formalization argues that reliable proof verification is a bottleneck for training and evaluating AI systems on hard mathematical reasoning: fully formal proofs in languages such as Lean are unambiguous and modular and therefore easy to check, but most proofs — particularly those written by AI systems — have neither property, and the formal libraries needed to express modern research mathematics remain far from complete. The authors define a proof format whose modules each state premises, conclusion, and proof in natural language, and pair it with Block Verification (BV), in which an LLM translates a proof into that format, verifies each module in isolation, and lets a calibrator model aggregate the per-module reports under a configurable strictness threshold. PF+BV Pareto-dominates LLM-as-judge baselines on error-finding precision and recall across olympiad and research-level mathematics, and the paper releases ArxivMathGradingBench as its research-level test bed.

## Tasks

Two benchmarks. Hard2Verify supplies 200 proofs generated by frontier AI models on 80 olympiad-level and Putnam problems, paired with step-level binary correctness labels. ArxivMathGradingBench, introduced here, holds 35 mathematics research papers with 40 known errors in total, built by querying the arXiv API for all math submissions between 2025-01-01 and 2025-07-30, regex-filtering revision comments for correction words alongside a mention of a lemma, theorem or proposition, classifying the survivors with GPT-5.4 nano, and reducing 100 candidates to 35 after removing non-permissively-licensed papers and false positives.

## Domains

Mathematical proof verification: olympiad and Putnam competition problems, and published research mathematics drawn from arXiv math categories.

## Evaluation

- **Step-level precision and recall.** On Hard2Verify, predicted incorrect step indices are pooled across the dataset into TP, FP and FN counts, from which precision and recall over erroneous steps are computed.
- **Proof-level precision and recall.** The same counts recomputed after mapping a proof to incorrect whenever any of its steps is flagged.
- **Coverage and false errors per proof.** Coverage is the fraction of flawed proofs in which every ground-truth error is flagged; false errors per proof is the mean number of predictions matching no annotated error.
- **Error-location matching.** On ArxivMathGradingBench a verifier receives the paper PDF and returns a list of error locations, and GPT-5.4 mini judges whether each returned location matches a ground-truth location, since papers offer no natural notion of a step.
- **Reported.** Main results use GPT-5.4 mini, with GPT-5.4 nano and full also swept. On Hard2Verify, PF+BV and the LLM-as-judge baseline both recover ~90% of erroneous steps, and PF+BV flags 60% fewer false steps per proof at k = 8. On ArxivMathGradingBench, both leave ~50% of annotated errors uncaught at k = 8, with PF+BV reaching higher recall, raising fewer false alarms, and covering every annotated error in a larger fraction of papers. No human baseline is reported.

## Typical Duration

Verification effort is reported as a compute budget rather than a duration: k = 8 independent runs per item, at 64K input / 26K output tokens per rollout on Hard2Verify and 213K input / 96K output tokens per rollout on ArxivMathGradingBench, for total API costs of $264.24 and $165.68. Not stated: no per-task wall-clock or step budget is given.

## Main Contribution

A proof format that decomposes a natural-language argument into self-contained modules stating premises, conclusion and proof, paired with a Block Verification algorithm that checks each module independently and a research-level verification benchmark built from author-corrected arXiv papers.

## Key Design Ideas

- Proof modules that each state their premises, conclusion and proof in natural language, organized by an invocation DAG and a scope-inheritance forest.
- Block verification in reverse topological order, so each module is checked with its children assumed correct.
- A separate calibrator model that aggregates per-module error reports into a verdict under a natural-language strictness threshold.
- Pessimistic aggregation over k parallel runs, with k swept to trace a precision–recall operating curve.

## Strengths

- Module-local checking keeps every verification call in a short-context regime instead of holding an entire proof at once.
- Error labels mined from authors' own arXiv revision comments supply research-level ground truth without new expert annotation.
- Every stage calls an off-the-shelf model with no fine-tuning, so the reported gain is attributable to the proof format rather than to a trained verifier.

## Limitations

- Repository note: Ground truth covers only the error locations authors disclosed in revision comments, so a genuine but unannotated error is counted as a false positive — the paper accordingly reports its precision values as lower bounds and its false-positive counts as upper bounds.
- Repository note: Precision and recall are published as curves over k rather than as tabulated point values, and ArxivMathGradingBench holds 35 papers, so numerical comparison against other trajectory-level evaluations rests on a small sample read from figures.
