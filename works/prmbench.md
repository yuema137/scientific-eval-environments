## Overview

PRMBench is a benchmark that assesses the fine-grained error-detection capabilities of process-level reward models (PRMs) on multi-step reasoning chains. It comprises 6,216 problems carrying 83,456 step-level labels, spread across nine error sub-categories grouped under simplicity, soundness, and sensitivity.

## Summary

PRMBench argues that current benchmarks focus on step correctness alone and therefore cannot evaluate PRMs systematically, even though language models produce many distinct kinds of reasoning error. It curates 6,216 test instances with 83,456 step-level labels and organizes them into three evaluation categories — simplicity, soundness, and sensitivity — divided into nine sub-categories. Across 25 evaluated models, spanning open-source PRMs alongside open and proprietary LLMs prompted as critic models, the best overall score is 68.8 against 83.8 for human annotators.

## Tasks

6,216 test instances carrying 83,456 step-level labels, averaging 13.4 steps per instance. Each instance pairs a question with a step-by-step solution process in which particular steps carry ground-truth negative labels. Instances divide across nine sub-categories — non-redundancy and non-circular logic under simplicity; empirical soundness, step consistency, domain consistency, and confidence invariance under soundness; prerequisite sensitivity, deception resistance, and multi-solution consistency under sensitivity — holding 750–758 instances each except multi-solution consistency at 165.

## Domains

Mathematical reasoning over PRM800K-derived problems, extended in the appendix by PRMBench-STEM covering physics, chemistry, and biology.

## Evaluation

- **Step-level binary classification.** Each evaluated model assigns a step-level validity score and a step-level redundancy score to every step, and the model's own threshold converts those scores into binary per-step predictions.
- **Negative F1.** The F1 score computed over negatively labeled steps, taken as the direct measure of error-detection performance.
- **PRMScore.** A normalized score combining negative F1 and F1 as a weighted sum, with the weights set to maximize differentiation between models; random guessing scores 50.0.
- **Human baseline.** Three annotators holding bachelor's degrees, each responsible for three sub-category subsets of a 450-sample mini-test set built by drawing 50 instances per sub-category.
- **Reported.** Gemini-2.0-thinking-exp-1219 leads the 25 evaluated models at 68.8 overall against 83.8 for humans, and Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-72B is the strongest open-source PRM at 68.2; o1-mini and DeepSeek-R1 were scored on a 394-sample subset rather than the full set, and the average Somers' D correlation between PRMBench and Best-of-N evaluation is −0.05.

## Typical Duration

Single-pass scoring of a fixed solution process averaging 13.4 steps, with no interaction loop. Not stated: no per-instance wall-clock, step, or token budget is given; cost appears only as the stated reason for restricting o1-mini and DeepSeek-R1 to a 394-sample subset.

## Main Contribution

A fine-grained process-level benchmark that resolves PRM error detection into nine sub-categories under simplicity, soundness, and sensitivity, released alongside an automated evaluation framework and customizable data-generation system.

## Key Design Ideas

- Three evaluation categories — simplicity, soundness, and sensitivity — subdivided into nine error sub-categories, so results resolve by error type rather than by aggregate step correctness.
- Error injection over PRM800K: completely correct solutions are selected from its train and test splits, then rewritten by GPT-4o under prompts specific to each target error class.
- Style-controlled curation intended to hold evaluation samples at consistent difficulty and mitigate confounding variables.
- A multi-solution consistency subset carrying no erroneous steps, built from alternative correct solution paths generated by QwQ-32B-Preview.

## Strengths

- Error-type resolution localizes a PRM's weakness to a named failure mode instead of collapsing it into one step-accuracy figure.
- Perturbing verified-correct PRM800K solutions yields exact ground-truth step labels, and manual review of 10% of instances reports over 92% qualification on modification correctness.
- Near-zero Somers' D against Best-of-N outcomes indicates the benchmark separates models along an axis that outcome-level selection accuracy does not.

## Limitations

- Repository note: All main-benchmark instances derive from PRM800K and its mathematical questions; physics, chemistry, and biology coverage sits in the separately constructed PRMBench-STEM extension, reported only in the appendix.
- Repository note: Erroneous steps are synthesized by prompting GPT-4o to rewrite correct solutions, so the error distribution is the one the class-specific prompts induce rather than one sampled from observed model failures.
